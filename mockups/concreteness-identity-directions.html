Concrete Curing: Why It Matters and How to Do It Right

By Geraldo Monte · 8 August 2026 · Concrete construction

Curing is the most underrated step in concrete construction. It is also the one most often skipped, rushed, or done badly — because by the time the failure shows up, the forms are long gone and the crew has moved to the next job. But curing decides whether the surface of your concrete is hard, dense and durable, or dusty, cracked and permeable.

Standard says

NZS 3101.1:2006 and Concrete NZ's Guide to Concrete Construction require a minimum of 7 days of continuous moisture retention for ordinary Portland cement concrete at moderate temperatures. Durability-critical elements are routinely specified at 14 days or more.

In practice

Measure the curing period from placement, not from when someone gets around to it. Interrupted curing — wet for two days, dry for two, wet again — is far less effective than continuous curing. Formwork retention is free curing: don't strip early to chase the programme.

Field note

On a bridge deck job, skipping the cure to hold the programme came up more than once. We held it. The cylinders passed, the surface stayed sound, and nobody remembers the two days we "lost" — but everyone would have remembered a dusty, cracked deck.

Frequently asked questions

How long should concrete be cured?

A minimum of 7 days of continuous moisture retention is standard practice for ordinary Portland cement concrete at moderate temperatures. Longer curing (14–28 days) improves durability, and hot, windy or dry conditions require more aggressive curing, not less.
