AI Is Now Your Customer: What GEO Means for Construction

By Geraldo Monte · 8 August 2026 · Industry trend

Here is a scenario that should worry every contractor. A client needs concrete repair work on a commercial building. They don't Google "concrete repair contractor" anymore. They open ChatGPT or Perplexity and ask: "Who is the best concrete repair contractor in Hamilton, New Zealand?" The AI answers with three names. If your company is not one of them, the conversation is over before it started — you never even knew it happened.

This is not speculation about the future. The shift is happening now, and it has a name: Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) — making your content discoverable and citable by AI assistants, the way SEO made it discoverable by search engines.

The numbers behind the shift

The data that convinced me comes from a podcast episode on AI visibility (Silicon Valley Girl, "How to Rank #1 in AI", August 2026), and it matches what marketing firms are reporting independently:

  • 35% of US consumers now start shopping inside an AI assistant rather than a search engine.
  • Conversion from ChatGPT recommendations: 15.9%, versus 1.76% from organic Google traffic — an order of magnitude.
  • AI-assisted shopping conversion is up 693% year over year.
  • 92% of marketers say they plan to optimize for AI search, but only 40% are actually doing it. The gap between intent and action is the first-mover window — and it is open now.

Construction tends to adopt marketing shifts late. That is exactly why the window matters here: while most contractors still think in terms of Google rankings and brochures, the clients who are asking AI for recommendations are already being answered — by whoever bothered to be findable.

What AI assistants recommend, and how

AI assistants don't return ten blue links. They return a synthesized answer — typically three to five names — built from the sources they can read and trust. The practical consequences for a contractor:

  • If your website content can't be read by AI crawlers (text hidden behind JavaScript, no transcripts, no structured data), you are invisible by default.
  • If your identity markers are weak (no structured data saying who you are, what you do, where you work), AI doesn't know what you do even when it reads your content.
  • If your content doesn't answer the questions clients actually ask, it won't be selected — even if it is technically readable.

In other words: the AI is a new customer that reads your website before your human customer ever does. If it can't read you, it can't recommend you.

The five-step GEO playbook, applied to a construction business

The method that worked for a podcast brand (going from invisible to fifth-most-visible among 11 tracked brands in two months) translates directly to a contractor's website:

Step 1 — Diagnose: can AI even read you?

Ask ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity: "Recommend a concrete repair contractor in [your region]", "who does seismic strengthening in Waikato?", "how much does concrete repair cost in NZ?" — then check whether you appear. Also ask the AI directly: "can you read this website?" Most sites fail this test quietly: the content exists, but crawlers can't extract it.

Step 2 — Build a machine-readable site

Static HTML pages that load completely before any interaction. Full text in plain HTML — no content locked behind JavaScript, accordions, or app-style interfaces. Transcripts of videos and podcasts as plain text. This single change gave one publisher 8,000–15,000 additional readable words per episode — every one of them attributable to the brand.

Step 3 — Fix your structured data and identity

Schema.org markup — Organization, Person, LocalBusiness, Article, FAQPage, BreadcrumbList — tells AI who you are, what you do, where you operate, and who wrote what. Identity markers in structured databases (Schema.org, Wikipedia, Google Business Profile) shape how AI categorizes your business. If these say the wrong thing — or nothing — AI doesn't know what you do even when it reads your content.

Step 4 — Optimize for the queries, not the keywords

AI answers are built from content that directly answers questions. Write pages that answer the real questions: "How much does concrete curing take?", "What causes concrete spalling?", "How do you repair spalled concrete?", "Who does bridge strengthening in NZ?" FAQ sections, how-to guides and plain-language answers are the content AI pulls from — and they are also exactly what clients want to read.

Step 5 — Track it monthly

Re-run the diagnosis queries every month and log whether you appear, in which AI, and how high. The tools change fast — what works in ChatGPT may lag in Perplexity or Claude. Track your own before/after, the way the podcast did (visibility doubled in two months). What gets measured gets fixed.

What this means for concrete and construction

Three implications, in order of urgency:

  1. Your website is now a sales document for AI. If it's a PDF-heavy brochure site or a JavaScript app, it is effectively invisible. The fix is cheap and permanent.
  2. Technical content is a moat. Construction has an advantage most industries don't: genuinely useful technical content (how-to guides, standards explanations, field lessons) is exactly what AI assistants need and what competitors rarely publish. The contractor who publishes it becomes the source AI cites.
  3. The window is now. Most competitors are not doing this. In construction specifically, almost none are. Two years from now, the sites that rank in AI answers will be the ones that started when the gap was open.

Why this hub exists

This page — and this whole hub — is the playbook being applied in practice. Every article is a static HTML page with full text, structured data, and answers to real questions. It is written for the client who asks AI who to call, and for the AI that answers them. Whether you found this page through a chatbot or a browser, the strategy is the same: be readable, be identifiable, be useful — and be early.

The bottom line

The biggest threat to a construction business in the next few years isn't AI replacing the work. It's AI ignoring the business. The fix is not complicated and it is not expensive — it is a machine-readable site, clear identity, and content that answers the questions clients actually ask. The contractors who do it while the window is open will be the names on the list. The rest will wonder why the phone stopped ringing.
